Episode Eleven · Aberdour Harbour, Fife

A listed harbour that fills and empties with the tide.

A listed harbour pier at Aberdour, on the Fife shore of the Firth of Forth, 17.3 rail miles from Edinburgh Waverley by the route over the Forth Bridge.

The real record

Aberdour is in Fife, on the north shore of the Firth of Forth, and the harbour is on Shore Road. We always say “Aberdour Harbour, Fife”: there is a second Aberdour, in Aberdeenshire, and the two are easy to confuse in a search.

Historic Environment Scotland (HES) lists the harbour pier as Category B, as “Aberdour, Harbour Pier including Workshop/Club House”, listed on 2 May 1973 (LB3595). The listing dates the harbour structure to the early 18th century, extended in the 19th century.

The listing also records that in 1703 there was a quay at Aberdour for loading coal and salt, and that in the following year the port was granted by charter to the Earl of Morton. It says a quay at Aberdour, not that this pier stands on it, and it names no particular earl.

Aberdour station, on the Fife Circle line on the route over the Forth Bridge from Edinburgh Waverley, opened in June 1890 and is listed Category B (LB3629). Our own route from Waverley to Aberdour came to 17.3 rail miles, taken from the route we drew on the map. It is not a published line mileage.

What you can see

Items marked “on our visit” are from our own filming on 23 September and 6 October 2026, not from a source.

A

The tide

On our visit the harbour dried out at low tide and filled at high tide: boats were aground on the slip at low water on 23 September, and moored afloat at full tide on 6 October. That is what the footage shows on those two days, not a statement of how often or how far the tide goes out.

B

The boat club

A dinghy marked “ABERDOUR BOAT CLUB” was moored in the harbour. We also filmed a building carrying the letters “ABC” and a boat-club plaque and burgee on a white building. We have not established whether those are one building or two, and we do not say either is the listed club house.

The club's own accountAberdour Boat Club says its regatta dates back to at least 1857, and that the club took the regatta over in 1953. It says the club itself was founded in November 1930. The board on site also gives 1857. These are the club's statements, and we say nothing about the regatta today.
C

The name

An interpretation board on site, “The Prospect From Aberdour”, says the name Aberdour is Pictish and means “mouth of the Dour”. We give that as the board's statement; we have not checked a place-name scholar.

D

Black Sands

A Fife Coast & Countryside Trust board says Aberdour Black Sands was awarded Scotland's Beach Award in 2026, and Welcome to Fife, the regional tourism body, lists it among Fife's 13 beaches that won 2026 awards. Those are two Fife bodies repeating one award, not two separate awarding authorities.

As of 6 October 2026The same board says Aberdour Harbour (Black Sands) is a designated bathing water monitored by SEPA, with “Excellent” bathing water quality. SEPA's own profile confirms the designation (2010); its page gives no current classification, so the “Excellent” is the board's.
