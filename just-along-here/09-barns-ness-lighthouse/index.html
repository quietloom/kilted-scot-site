Episode Seven · Barns Ness Lighthouse, East Lothian

A lighthouse that is no longer a light.

A tower built in 1899 to 1901 to a design by David A. Stevenson, decommissioned in 2005 and later sold to the cement company next door. It stands on a protected coast, about 50 minutes by car and 32.6 miles from Edinburgh Waverley.

The real record

Barns Ness Lighthouse was built between 1899 and 1901. The doorway lintel carries the date 1901, and the same lintel reads “D A STEVENSON, ENGINEER”. Historic Environment Scotland's listing (LB1465) names him alone as the designer, and so does the listing text reproduced by British Listed Buildings.

David A. Stevenson was a cousin of Robert Louis Stevenson. The lighthouse's former keepers' cottages stand beside it: Historic Environment Scotland lists a pair of single-storey cottages.

Our own route from Edinburgh Waverley to Barns Ness came to 32.6 miles and about 50 minutes by car (a Google Maps check by the owner gave 49 minutes and 32.9 miles). These are free-flow figures, so treat them as “about”.

What happened to it, and what is round it

01

Decommissioned, then sold

The light was decommissioned in 2005. It was later sold to the cement company next door, Lafarge.

Worth knowingSources disagree on when the sale happened: a trade article gives 2006 and some retellings give 2007. This page gives no year. We also make no claim about who owns it now.
02

A protected coast

The Barns Ness coast is a Site of Special Scientific Interest. The board on site says so, and so does Wikipedia's list of East Lothian SSSIs.

03

Fossils

The coast is rich in fossils. The board on site calls it “one of the best places in Scotland to find fossils”; the Scottish Geology Trust's page on Barns Ness is the second source.

04

The walk

The Scottish Geology Trust says the short coastal walk from the White Sands car park to the lighthouse follows “arguably the best exposures of limestones in east central Scotland”.

Attributed to one sourceThis is the Trust's statement, and we have found no second source for it. It is about the walk, not the lighthouse.
