Episode Twelve · Kirkcaldy War Memorial Gardens, Fife

A memorial park given in memory of a son.

The war memorial and gardens at Kirkcaldy, in Fife, 28.0 miles by road from Edinburgh Waverley. The land was gifted by John Nairn, and the cenotaph's bronze plaque is dedicated to the men of Kirkcaldy who gave their lives in the Great War.

The real record

Kirkcaldy War Memorial Gardens are at Bennochy Road and Abbotshall Road, in Kirkcaldy, Fife. Historic Environment Scotland (HES) lists the war memorial and gardens, with their sundial, gatepiers and quadrant walls, as Category B (LB44002), added on 27 February 1997. The museum (LB44000) and the Adam Smith Halls (LB44003) are separate HES listings.

The cenotaph carries a bronze plaque that reads “TO THE ABIDING MEMORY OF THOSE MEN OF KIRKCALDY WHO GAVE THEIR LIVES IN THE GREAT WAR THIS PARK AND ITS BUILDINGS ARE DEDICATED”. We read it from our own photograph, and the Imperial War Museums' register records the same inscription.

The land was gifted by John Nairn and was the site of Balsusney House, according to Fife Council. The cairn plaque we photographed says the memorial site was gifted by John Nairn in 1925. The Imperial War Museums (IWM) says the linoleum manufacturer John Nairn bequeathed the buildings and garden to the town as a war memorial, in memory of his son. OnFife says he was chairman of Michael Nairn & Co, and that the gift was made in memory of his only son Ian, who was killed in action in France a few months before the end of the First World War.

Our own route from Edinburgh Waverley to the gardens came to 28.0 miles by road.

Two memorials, and what the sources say about them

01

The First World War memorial

HES describes it as dating from 1923: a stepped ashlar cenotaph with bronzes to three faces, and flanking walls each with 18 bronze commemorative plaques. The IWM records that Admiral of the Fleet Lord Wester Wemyss unveiled it on 27 June 1925, and OnFife says the extended war memorial and galleries were opened in 1925.

Story vs recordWho designed it is not settled. HES and the IWM credit different people, and they may be describing different elements, so this page names no designer.
02

The Second World War memorial

HES describes it as dating from 1954: a white sarcophagus tomb on a chamfered plinth, with a quadrant wall behind it, piers dated 1939 and 1945, 11 bronze commemorative plaques, and a frieze inscribed GREATER LOVE HATH NO MAN THAN THIS. HES attributes it to Williamson and Hubbard.

Worth knowingHES gives no unveiling date for this memorial, so this page gives none.

What you can see

Items marked “on our visit” are from our own filming on 7 October 2026, not from a source.

A

The wall and the sarcophagus

On our visit the piers of the Second World War memorial wall read 1939 and 1945, with the frieze GREATER LOVE HATH NO MAN THAN THIS above and the sarcophagus in front. A separate stone cope on the memorial wall read OTHER CONFLICTS. We do not give the names of the dead, which were not legible in our footage.

B

The poppies

OnFife's centenary-year review says a community effort covered the memorial and the balcony of the Galleries in more than 8,000 knitted poppies, many made by the CastOn knitting and crochet group. It gives no date for the covering beyond the centenary year, 2025. On 7 October 2026 knitted red poppies still covered the cenotaph, the museum balcony and the Second World War sarcophagus. We do not know how long they stay.

C

The museum and gallery

The museum frontage carries the lettering MUSEUM AND ART GALLERY; HES names the building Kirkcaldy Museum, Art Gallery and Public Library. Kirkcaldy Galleries is run by Fife Cultural Trust, operating as OnFife, and the entrance sign reads ON AT KIRKCALDY GALLERIES and WAR MEMORIAL GARDENS. We say nothing about opening hours or exhibitions.

D

The royal visit and the cairn

Fife Council says King Charles III and Queen Camilla visited Kirkcaldy on Wednesday 2 July 2025 for the centenary of the war memorial, and that the King unveiled a new commemorative cairn. The cairn plaque we photographed names both.

E

The Normandy stone

A stone marks the 1944 Normandy Campaign. It carries the words IN REMEMBRANCE OF ALL SERVICES WHO FOUGHT IN THE NORMANDY CAMPAIGN and WE WILL REMEMBER THEM, according to our photograph, and War Memorials Online records it as a commemorative stone for the campaign.

F

The bench

A metal bench with silhouette figures carries the words KIRKCALDY REMEMBERS and LEST WE FORGET. We found no source for who placed it or when, so we say nothing about that.

G

The oak doorway

An oak sculpture in the shape of a doorway stands in the gardens as a Holocaust memorial. The plaque on it says it is intended as a mark of respect for all victims of the Holocaust and of genocide, and carries the Adam Smith College and Fife Council logos. War Memorials Online has photographs of it dated October 2019, so it dates from 2019 or earlier. We found no maker or unveiling date.

H

Sir Sandford Fleming

A plaque in the gardens reads, in part, “SIR SANDFORD FLEMING 1827-1915. Inventor of Standard Time and pioneer in world communications, Fleming was born in Kirkcaldy”, and says he was made a Burgess and Freeman of the Town in 1882 and knighted in 1897. It was erected by the Archaeological and Historic Sites Board, Ministry of Colleges and Universities, Ontario, Canada. “Inventor of Standard Time” is the plaque's own wording. The Dictionary of Canadian Biography says he was born in Kirkcaldy on 7 January 1827, became chief engineer of the Pacific railway, and is associated with the system of one-hour time zones.

I

The Adam Smith Halls

A separate building with its own HES listing (LB44003). HES says the Halls were opened on 11 October 1899 by Andrew Carnegie. A round Kirkcaldy Civic Society plaque on the building reads ADAM SMITH HALLS, Planned by Provost Michael Beveridge (1835-90), Opened in 1899 by Andrew Carnegie, in recognition of Kirkcaldy's Greatest Citizen ADAM SMITH (1723-90), Author of 'Wealth of Nations'. The phrase “Greatest Citizen” is the Civic Society's wording, not ours. OnFife says the Adam Smith Theatre is named after the Kirkcaldy-born economist and philosopher.
