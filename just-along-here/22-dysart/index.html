Episode Ten · Dysart, Fife

A 1600s harbour under the cliff, on the Fife coast.

A harbour under a sandstone crag in Fife, 30.3 miles by road from Edinburgh Waverley. It was dressed as Le Havre for series 2 of Outlander, and the records disagree about the tunnel beside it.

The real record

Historic Environment Scotland (HES), in its listing for the harbour (LB36406), says Dysart was a port in 1450, and the Fife Coast and Countryside Trust (FCCT) brochure says it was recorded as a port as early as 1450. HES describes an early 17th-century tidal basin. It quotes a 1997 history, by Swan and McNeill, saying the new inner dock, closed by dock gates, let ships load coal at all stages of the tide and was the first harbour on the east coast of Scotland to have such a facility. That superlative is the 1997 book's, quoted by HES, not HES's own.

Dysart Harbour was portrayed as the French port of Le Havre in series 2 of Outlander, according to Welcome to Fife, the FCCT and Find That Location. The set was dressed and digitally altered, so the harbour did not look like this on screen.

Dysart Sailing Club leases the harbour from Fife Council: HES says the club took it over after 1967 and leases it from the local authority, and the Courier said the same in October 2024.

HES lists the Tolbooth and Town Hall on the High Street and Victoria Street as Category A (LB36418). It dates the tower 1576, with a forestair dated 1617. HES also lists 1 Pan Ha' (Bay House) as Category A, dated 1583 and reconstructed in 1969, with the National Trust for Scotland Little Houses Scheme instrumental in the reconstruction. That entry covers that one house only.

Canmore says St Serf's Church was built in about 1500 and abandoned in 1802, and that its tower “may” have served as a watch-tower in the area's coastal defences. The Victorian St Serf's Church on West Port is a different building.

The FCCT brochure says the renovated Harbourmaster's House was officially opened in September 2006. HES lists Harbour House on Hot Pot Wynd as Category B, previously the Harbour Master's House and warehouse. The name Hot Pot Wynd probably means “hot pit”; the Fife Place-name Data at the University of Glasgow says the Old Statistical Account records underground coal fires there, with flames visible at night from the pit mouths. The database names no particular fire.

HES's record for the Dysart House designed landscape says a building at the harbour processed whale oil in 1835, and that Lord Rosslyn, affected by the smell, took action in the House of Lords and won.

Our own route from Edinburgh Waverley to Dysart Harbour came to 30.3 miles by road.

Things the sources disagree on

01

The Tolbooth's rebuild

HES's listing says the upper stages were wrecked by an explosion in 1656 and not rebuilt until 1707, while its own description says the belfry was rebuilt in 1743–4. HES gives two answers, so this page gives no rebuild date.

02

The path tunnel

A Fife Council committee paper of 22 April 2025 says the tunnel runs through a sandstone crag to the Inner Harbour and was built as a rail tunnel for moving ballast stones to ships. The FCCT says rails were laid for trucks to take away ballast that was blocking the harbour. The sources disagree on the direction.

Not the same tunnelHES records a separate Lady Violet Tunnel, opened in 1892 by the wife of the 5th Earl of Rosslyn as part of a failed 10,000-pound coal scheme. HES says it runs under the terraces of Dysart House. Nothing we found shows it is the path tunnel, and we do not say it is.
03

Sea Beams

Sea Beams is a group of nine oak beams by the artist Donald Urquhart, part of the Dysart Artworks project. The sources differ on the details. One gives the height as 7 to 9 metres above ground and another as 30 feet, so this page gives no height. On the colours, City Design's project account says they come from photographs of the Firth of Forth taken by local residents; the Fife Art Trail says shades of blue matching the sky.

What you can see

Items marked “on our visit” are from our own filming on 13 and 19 October 2026, not from a source.

A

The harbour

On our visit we saw yachts and small boats moored under the sandstone crag, with the harbour's stone quay walls around them.

B

The Tolbooth, Pan Ha' and St Serf's tower

On our visit we saw the Tolbooth with its tower, the Pan Ha' houses by the shore, and St Serf's tower on the green, with a Lang Toun Coast information board at its foot. We name no house number for any Pan Ha' house in our footage.

C

Sea Beams

On our visit we photographed the Dysart Town Trail board and a Sea Beams information board. The beams themselves were fenced when we visited. Our owner counted nine, one partly hidden behind another.

D

The sailing club's figure

On our visit we saw the Dysart Sailing Club's donations figure, a diver with a collecting box.

E

The tunnel and the pier end

On our visit we filmed the coastal path tunnel and the views from the bench at the pier end. Fife Council said on 4 November 2025 that, after cliff-stabilisation work west of the harbour, the coastal path had been restored to its original route through the harbour tunnel. We have not re-checked its current status on site. The island on the horizon is Inchkeith; that is our own identification.

F

The Dysart Cat

The Dysart Cat, by Yolande Kenny (yolandekenny.com), was sited at Dysart Pan Ha' during the 2026 Wemyss Ware Cat Trail. According to the trail website it was one of 17 cat sculptures, and the trail ran from 25 April to 6 September 2026. The trail has ended.

Attributed to one sourceThe trail's own website is the only source we found for the cat's siting and the dates.
G

Heritage trails

Fife Today reports that Fife Council launched heritage trails with an app in June 2026 covering Burntisland, Dysart and Kirkcaldy, part of a 150,000-pound programme from the Community Recovery Fund, and that the app was made by Peel Associates. The 150,000 pounds covers the whole programme, not the panels. We have not found who made the Lang Toun Coast panels and do not say.

H

Wee Walter

Walter Nachtigall, a boy from Vienna who came to Britain on a Kindertransport, was hosted in Dysart by Jimmy and Isa Salmond and their children, according to his own oral history at the US Holocaust Memorial Museum and to Fife Today in March 2022. The sources differ on when, so we give no date. Fife Today's wording for the stay is “He came for a week, and stayed for a year”.
